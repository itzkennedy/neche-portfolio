About

Founder and software developer. I find where a business is getting stuck and build the software that removes the constraint.

I'm a founder and software developer. I build the systems behind business processes, not just the pages in front of them. I work across frontend, backend, automation and AI-assisted systems. Most of what I take on begins as a problem someone can describe in one sentence: the work is manual, the information is scattered or the customers are slipping away.

I built my first website - a football transfer site - in 2022 to see whether I could build for the web at all. A trading bot for Deriv's synthetic indices followed in 2024; it was the first software where the logic had to be correct, because mistakes showed up in the output, not just on the screen. From there I built websites for different artists and businesses, then worked as a frontend developer at Orgebyte, building production web applications with React and Next.js in a remote team with regular code review.

The work then moved from building what I was asked to build to building around problems. MedTrack replaced manual dose tracking during my internship at APIN, with separate patient and caregiver experiences. I founded BAYA, a student marketplace, because campus buying and selling was failing in WhatsApp groups; it now runs on campus verification, listings and in-app messaging. And Vulcan, an AI coding agent, audits a codebase for bugs and makes corrections from plain-English instructions.

I don't start from a stack. I start from the constraint - what the business does now, what that costs and what changes if the work is done by software instead. Then I build the smallest thing that actually removes the constraint and keep improving it once it is in use. Websites, web apps, internal tools and AI systems are only the forms that answer takes. I build both my own products and software for other organisations; the approach is the same either way.

How I think about software

I don't start with a technology and look for somewhere to use it. I start with the problem. What is taking too long? What is difficult to manage? Where are people losing information or customers? Once I understand that, I work out whether software is actually the right solution and what the simplest useful version should look like.

  • Identify the business problem, not the feature request.
  • Understand the process causing it and put a rough number on what it costs.
  • Decide whether software is the right answer - often it is not.
  • Build the smallest system that removes the constraint, then improve it.

I'm always open to a business problem worth solving. If you have one - a process run by hand, information hard to find or customers working too hard - let's talk.
