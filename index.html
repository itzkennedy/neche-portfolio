What I solve

The problems worth writing software for.

Software is the tool, not the goal. These are the business problems I know how to take on and what building the fix usually involves.

  1. 01

    Work that keeps costing you time

    Repetitive work done by hand quietly drains hours from a business. Your team re-enters the same data, checks the same information, follows up with the same people and moves information from one system to another. A task that takes three hours every day becomes 780 hours a year. That is almost 20 full working weeks spent repeating one process. And that is before counting the interruptions, mistakes and work that gets delayed because someone is stuck doing it manually.

    What it can cost A task that takes 3 hours a day, 5 days a week, adds up to about 780 hours a year. At $15 an hour, that is $11,700 of working time spent on one recurring task. If several people are involved, the cost multiplies quickly. Software can handle the repetition so your team spends its time on work that actually requires judgement.

  2. 02

    Customers who never make it to the finish line

    A customer can arrive ready to buy, book or enquire and still disappear before completing the process. They click the wrong thing. They wait too long for a response. They cannot find the information they need. They abandon the form. They send a message and never hear back.

    Nobody sends you an invoice for that lost customer. The revenue simply never happens.

    The math If 50,000 qualified prospects reach your business and 2% become customers, that gives you 1,000 customers.
    If the right software removes the friction keeping those prospects from completing the journey, even a significant improvement in conversion can create substantial additional revenue.
    At an average customer value of $100, moving from 2% to 6% conversion would mean 3,000 additional customers and $300,000 in additional revenue from the same 50,000 prospects.
    That is revenue your business may already be paying to acquire but losing before the customer completes the journey.
    Good software should not just cost your business money. It should remove expensive problems and create enough value to justify the investment many times over.

  3. 03

    Information your team can't easily use

    Important information is scattered across WhatsApp groups, notebooks, spreadsheets, emails and people's memories. Someone asks for an update and the answer is buried in a conversation from three weeks ago. Someone works from an outdated spreadsheet. Someone has information that nobody else knows exists.

    The business keeps moving, but the information needed to make good decisions is constantly being searched for, copied, checked and reconstructed.

    That time adds up. And so do the mistakes.

    Software can bring the important information into one system where the right people can access the current version when they need it.

  4. 04

    Ideas that need to become real products

    An idea can sit in a notebook, WhatsApp chat or business plan for months because there is no working product behind it.

    The problem is rarely the lack of another idea. It is the gap between knowing what should exist and actually building it.

    A product needs more than a concept. It needs the right flow, working interfaces, data, logic and a way for real people to use it.

    I take a clearly defined problem and turn it into working software that can be tested, used and improved once it is live.
